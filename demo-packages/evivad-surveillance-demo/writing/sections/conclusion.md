\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented \evivad{}, a unified framework that addresses three real problems exposed once large models enter surveillance video anomaly detection. Our starting point is a simple judgement: now that the training-free paradigm has established that large models are usable for VAD, the marginal return of further score chasing is falling quickly, while two questions---\emph{can an explanation be verified} and \emph{does the system degrade gracefully under input corruption}---are almost never answered systematically.

Around this judgement we propose three modules. DAA mitigates domain shift within a budget of fewer than $5$\,M trainable parameters through low-rank adaptation. EAD turns explanations into falsifiable assertions with evidence citations, trained by an InfoNCE evidence-alignment loss. DAG drives a continuous switch between two scoring paths via a lightweight quality probe and abstains explicitly under low confidence. In parallel, we promote explanation verifiability (EAR / CFS / HR / TCR) and degradation robustness (RPR / ECE) to evaluation dimensions on par with detection accuracy, and establish a unified protocol on UCF-Crime, three cross-domain sets and a $4\times3$ degradation grid. The aggregate results indicate that the three modules are complementary on disjoint dimensions and that a weak positive synergy emerges when they are combined.

\paragraph{Limitations.} First, the current package does not include traceable training records. Second, EAR and CFS rely on human annotation of minimal sufficient evidence sets, distractor evidence and counterfactual labels, which is costly and whose scalability remains to be tested. Third, DAG's quality labels are generated automatically from degradation type and severity, so distribution shift is possible for degradation types unseen during training. Fourth, our evaluation is still single-view; cross-camera view consistency is not yet incorporated.

\paragraph{Future work.} Three directions stand out. The first is \textbf{cross-dataset and cross-view generalisation}: extending DAA's ``one adapter per scene'' into a composable multi-scene adapter library and introducing cross-view evidence consistency constraints in multi-camera settings, so that conclusions do not depend on a single dataset. The second is \textbf{real-time inference and edge deployment}: combining edge--cloud collaboration with knowledge-graph reasoning to lift DAG's clip-level dynamic routing to device-level scheduling, deciding within a latency budget which clips deserve to be escalated to a large model. The third is \textbf{multimodal extension}: bringing audio, depth and thermal modalities into the evidence-slot system so that EAD's ``evidence'' is no longer limited to RGB temporal intervals but becomes a cross-modal, inspectable set of assertions. We believe that as explanation evaluation and robustness evaluation become community consensus, the evaluation of video anomaly detection will move from ``how high is the score'' to ``how trustworthy is the conclusion''.
